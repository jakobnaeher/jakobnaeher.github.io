About Loopkeeper

Loopkeeper keeps your place in a pattern: import a PDF or photo, drag the row marker to the line you are on, highlight repeats, and count rows with big counters that roll into repeat counters. Projects sync between your iPhone and iPad through your own iCloud.

Frequently asked

Do I need an account?

No. Loopkeeper uses your iCloud account for sync. There is no Loopkeeper account, no sign-up and no subscription.

Where are my patterns stored?

On your devices and in your private iCloud (CloudKit). Pattern files are never uploaded anywhere else and never shared with anyone. Loopkeeper does not re-host or redistribute patterns.

What is the row helper? (version 1.1 and later)

Type or paste a row or a section of your pattern, and the helper explains it step by step in plain words, lists the abbreviations, and works out how the stitch count changes. It can also propose counters for the piece (for example a repeat of 8 rows worked 12 times, with a reminder on row 5) and add them to your project with one tap. The row helper is part of the one-time unlock, with up to 200 questions in any 30 days and 800 in total.

My stitch count is off. Can the helper find out why? (version 1.2 and later)

Yes. Paste the row, tap “My stitch count is off” and enter the count you have (and, if you know them, the count the pattern expects and the count before the row). The helper works out what the row should end with and names the mistakes that would explain exactly that difference: where to look in your work and how to put it right, without ripping back when that is possible.

Can it adjust a pattern to my gauge? (version 1.2 and later)

Yes. Paste the piece, tap “Adjust it for my gauge” and enter the pattern’s gauge and yours. The helper only finds the stitch and row counts in the text; the app does the arithmetic and shows the sum, for example 124 × 20 ÷ 22 = 112.73, so you can check it. If the pattern states a multiple (“multiple of 8 + 4”), the suggestion keeps it. Anything that cannot be converted by multiplying, such as shaping, is pointed out for your own judgement.

Does the row helper see my pattern?

Only the text you type or paste into it, and the numbers you enter in the helper. After you agree on the permission screen, that text is sent to the Loopkeeper helper service and on to OpenAI, whose AI writes the answer. The helper service keeps none of it, and your pattern files are never sent. The details are in the privacy policy. You can withdraw the permission in Settings at any time.

Can counters be set up on my device? (version 1.5 and later)

On an iPhone or iPad with Apple Intelligence, yes. With the unlock, “Set up counters for it” then uses Apple’s on-device model: your text is not sent anywhere and no permission screen appears. The model only reads the numbers out of your text (rows in the repeat, how many times, rows to work, notes on rows); Loopkeeper checks each one against the text and builds the counters itself. A measurement such as 30 cm or a stitch count is never taken as a number of rows, and the helper tells you if it left something out. The other row helper functions work through the helper service as described above.

The helper says it isn’t available right now.

The helper service can’t answer at the moment. Your projects and patterns are untouched and nothing is taken from your allowance. Try again later; the rest of the app works as usual.

Can I trust the helper’s answers?

They are written by AI and are usually right, but they can be wrong. Check them against your pattern, which always has the last word. The helper says so when a piece of text is ambiguous or cut off.

Which abbreviations are explained without the helper? (version 1.4 and later)

As soon as you type or paste a row into the row helper, Loopkeeper lists the standard abbreviations in it (English and German knitting and crochet terms) with their meaning, from a list built into the app. This works offline, without the AI and without the unlock. Where US and UK crochet patterns use the same term for different stitches (dc, tr), it says so instead of guessing.

My stitch count is off and the helper isn’t available. (version 1.4 and later)

Open “My stitch count is off” and enter the count you have and the count the pattern says. Loopkeeper shows the exact difference at once and the usual things to check for it, such as a missed yarn over or a decrease worked twice. This works without the AI; “Find the likely cause” asks the helper to look at your row.

Can I convert a count to my gauge without AI? (version 1.5 and later)

Yes. In the row helper, open “Adjust it for my gauge”; no pattern text is needed. Enter the pattern’s gauge, yours and a stitch or row count from the pattern, and the app shows the sum and the number to use at once, for example 120 × 20 ÷ 22 = 109.09, use 109. This works offline, without the AI and without the unlock. If the pattern works in a repeat, use the nearest number that fits it. “Recalculate the numbers” still has the helper find every count in a pasted piece.

What is free and what is the unlock?

Free: two projects and one pattern per project, with full export. A single one-time purchase unlocks unlimited projects and patterns and the row helper. No subscription, ever.

I bought the unlock on my iPhone; my iPad says I haven't.

Open Settings → Restore purchase on the iPad. The unlock is tied to your Apple Account, so every device signed in with it can restore.

How does undo work with linked counters?

Undo takes back exactly your last tap on this device — including the rollover it caused on the linked repeat counter — and nothing else. If you also tapped the repeat counter directly in between, that change stays.

Can I back everything up or move to a new device?

Yes. Settings → Export everything gives you a folder with every project as JSON plus every pattern file exactly as you imported it. On the new device, choose Import and pick the whole export folder. Projects already present on a device are left unchanged.

Does the row marker move by itself when I count?

No. A PDF has no idea where its rows are, so Loopkeeper never guesses: you drag the marker, Loopkeeper counts. The marker stays exactly where you put it.

I counted on two devices while offline. Will rows be lost?

No. Every tap is saved as its own small record, so rows counted on your iPhone and your iPad while they were apart are all kept once they sync. If both devices stepped a counter down past zero at the same time, the extra step is simply ignored.

Apple Watch?

Not in the first version. It is planned, and the counter design is prepared for it.
