WhenWeGo — Privacy Policy

What the app stores, what it shares, and what it never does.

SupportPrivacyAll apps

This policy describes what WhenWeGo does with data. It is written from the app's actual behaviour and is kept short on purpose.

What WhenWeGo stores and where

Trips, participants and plan data are stored on your devices and in iCloud using Apple's CloudKit: a trip you organize lives in your private iCloud database; when you share it, its contents are placed in a shared CloudKit zone that the people you invite can read (and, with edit access, change). Your exact budget figure is stored only in your private iCloud database and only published to the shared trip if you choose to share it. The developer cannot access your trips.

What is shared with the group

On a shared trip, everyone with access sees: trip name, dates, destinations, proposals and votes, stays, travel legs, itinerary items, shared expenses, and for each participant their name, departure city and airports, interests, free and blocked date ranges, flexibility, travel-time and connection limits, accessibility notes, lodging preference and budget level (1–5). Share trips with people you trust: iCloud sharing grants read or read-write access to the whole trip, and the app's finer rules (who may edit what) are enforced by the app, not by the server.

Network requests

To compute typical weather, WhenWeGo sends a selected destination's coordinates and historical full-month date ranges to Open-Meteo (archive-api.open-meteo.com). These requests reveal the month being compared, but do not send your name, exact future trip days, budget or device location. Open-Meteo's free-service policy says it may collect IP addresses for technical maintenance and misuse prevention, and keeps troubleshooting logs that may contain geographical coordinates for up to 90 days. The provider states that this information is not linked to user identities and is not shared with third parties. WhenWeGo also requests a fixed current euro rate table from Frankfurter (api.frankfurter.dev), whose hosted-service policy states that it does not retain individual request/IP/URL logs. Place search uses Apple Maps (MapKit); invitation and sync traffic uses Apple's iCloud. See Open-Meteo's policy and Frankfurter's policy.

Assistant

The optional assistant runs on your device with Apple's Foundation Models (Apple Intelligence). No prompt or trip data is sent to WhenWeGo or to any AI provider by the assistant. (The Planner, described below, is a separate feature.)

The Planner (version 1.1 and later, optional purchase)

The Planner suggests places to stay, routes, a day-by-day plan and destinations with the help of artificial intelligence and web search. It sends nothing until you have agreed on its permission screen, and then only when you ask it for something.

What is sent: a description of the trip without names. Each participant is sent as “Traveller 1”, “Traveller 2” and so on, with their home city and airports, travel-time and connection limits, interests, budget level (1–5, never an amount), accommodation preference, whether they share accommodation, and any accessibility notes they entered. Also sent: the chosen destination, the dates or the search window, the other destinations under consideration when you ask where to go, the stays, travel legs and itinerary already saved (with names replaced by the same numbers), anything you type into the Planner’s wish field, and your language. With each request the app also sends the App Store’s signed record of your Planner purchase, so the service can check that the request comes from a purchased copy.

What is never sent: names, exact budget figures, votes, expenses, and the contact details of the people on the iCloud share.

Where it goes: to the WhenWeGo planner service, which the developer runs on servers of Supabase in the United States, and from there to OpenAI (OpenAI, L.L.C., United States), whose model searches the web and writes the suggestions. The planner service keeps no trip data: it passes the request on, returns the answer to your device and stores only a usage counter under a one-way hash of your purchase’s transaction number. Like any hosting provider, Supabase may keep technical request logs such as time and IP address under its own terms. OpenAI states that by default it does not use data sent through its API to train its models, and that it retains such data for up to 30 days to monitor for abuse. To search the web, OpenAI’s model sends search queries it composes from the request (for example a destination and the kind of place) to web search.

Other people’s details: a trip contains details of everyone in the group. Use the Planner only if the others are fine with that; the permission screen says so.

What the Planner is not: it never knows prices, timetables or availability and it books nothing. Its suggestions are written by AI from web pages and can be wrong or out of date; each named business comes with the page it was found on.

Your control: Trip settings → Withdraw permission for the Planner stops any further sending until you agree again.

What the developer collects

WhenWeGo has no analytics, advertising or tracking code, and stores nothing of yours on a developer-operated server. Because of the Planner, the App Store privacy label also lists Purchase History (the transaction record that authorises Planner requests), and the trip description sent to the Planner falls under Other User Content; both are used only to make the feature work and are not used for tracking. The weather provider's retained request context and technical data are disclosed in the App Store privacy label as Other User Content and Other Diagnostic Data for App Functionality. The label conservatively marks these as linked to you because we cannot verify anonymization before collection; this does not mean that the developer receives your trips or that the provider claims to identify you. Neither category is used for tracking. If you email support, the developer receives your email and message and uses them to help you.

Permissions

WhenWeGo asks for no device permissions beyond iCloud (used for storage and sharing). It does not access your calendar, contacts, photos or location.

Deleting your data

Delete a trip you organize to remove it for everyone (Trip settings → Delete trip); this also deletes your private budget figure for it. Leave a trip you were invited to from Trip settings (Manage or leave this trip): your access ends and, once you confirm, the private budget figure you entered for that trip is deleted from your iCloud. Your participant entry (name, dates, stays, travel) remains in the organizer's trip until the organizer removes it under People. Deleting the app removes its local data; iCloud data is removed when you delete your trips or manage WhenWeGo's data in iOS Settings → Apple Account → iCloud.

Children

WhenWeGo is not directed at children under 13 and is rated for general audiences.

Changes

This policy is updated when the app's data practices change; the date at the bottom of the page is the current version.
